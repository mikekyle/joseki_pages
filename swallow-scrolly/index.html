Does the Swallow Still Fly?

We searched 120,000 pro games for a four-stone corner pattern — and asked KataGo what happens next.

Meet the Swallow — a corner sequence every Go player meets in the opening. Four stones, a familiar shape. But do pros still agree on what comes after?

The book line: D16, C14, C12, F16. Black and white trade influence in the top-left corner. This exact sequence showed up 1,159 times in our pro-game database.

We ran KataGo on 100 of those positions. The winrate for the player to move spreads across the whole board — mean 47.7%, median 49.2%. Roughly even, on average.

But "average" hides the drama. Some positions sit at 9% winrate. Others hit 84%. Same joseki, wildly different outcomes — context matters.

So what do pros actually play? D14 is the AI favourite (52% of #1 picks). F15 takes another 24%. The Swallow lands, but the flock splits.

The most common continuation after F15? G15, F14 — seen in 40% of follow-up sequences. Local fighting stays in the corner 79% of the time.

The Swallow still flies. The book sequence appears over a thousand times in pro play. But the landing strip has forked — and that's where the game really begins.
